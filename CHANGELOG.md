# Changelog

## 1.0 (current)

### Plans
- The Mac app looks like Apple Music: plans sit in the sidebar like playlists, with their first exercise's photo as artwork. A plan opens as a table of its exercises with sets, reps and weight to edit in place, and a search to find an exercise in it. Under Library, every exercise is listed with its muscles and equipment, to sort, search and add to a plan.
- On the Mac, the up and down arrow keys move between a plan's exercises and through the exercise library, with the preview following along.
- Move an exercise to another plan from its menu, on the Mac and the iPhone. It keeps its sets, reps and weight.
- On the Mac, All plans in the sidebar shows every plan side by side with what it trains and its exercises by name. Drag an exercise onto another plan to move it there, with its sets, reps and weight; click a plan to open it.
- See what changed in your plans, and when: across all plans on the Mac under Library → Changes and on the iPhone in Plans, and for one plan from its Changes button. Typing a weight or a name shows as one change. Undo any change from there while what it changed is still there: a removed exercise comes back with its sets, reps and weight, a deleted plan with its exercises. In a plan's own history, Restore brings the plan back to how it was after any change, your sets, reps and weights included; undo the restore to get today's plan back.
- Replace an exercise with another in the same place, from its menu, on the Mac and the iPhone. Sets and reps stay; the weight starts at your last one for the new exercise, else 0 kg.
- On the Mac, type a plan's sets and reps, or step to them.
- Build plans on the Mac from a searchable catalog of 650 exercises by RepDB, each with illustrations and step-by-step instructions, or add your own, each with target sets, reps and weight. Settings credits RepDB.
- Make your own exercises, and give any exercise a description, a video link and a photo. Renaming an exercise keeps its history.
- On the Mac, add exercises to a plan by describing them, typed or dictated, in English or German: "3x10 bench press at 60 kg, then squats". Needs Apple Intelligence.
- On the Mac, let Claude create and extend plans through MCP: switch it on in Settings, where the commands for Claude Code and Claude Desktop are. In a plan shared with your training partner, what Claude adds is yours alone unless you ask for both, or Settings says always both.
- Exercise search finds every word anywhere and ignores plurals and hyphens: "dumbbell curls" finds Dumbbell Bicep Curl.
- Every plan says what it trains, from its exercises' muscles and sets: Leg-heavy, Chest & Back, or Full body. It shows under the plan when you pick a workout, on the watch too, and on the Mac's plan page.
- In German, exercises are German too: their names, how to do them, and their muscles, equipment, level and category. Search finds an exercise by its English or German name. Plans and history from before the new catalog show their exercises as "Custom exercise". Your description, video link or photo for an exercise the new catalog doesn't have no longer lists as a blank exercise in the library, the exercise picker or Claude's search.
- Filter the exercise catalog by body region, level, equipment and category, like legs for beginners with dumbbells, when adding an exercise and in the Mac's exercise library.
- The Mac's exercise library previews the selected exercise on the right, like Finder: its photo, muscles and equipment, how to do it, and a video link. It opens when you select an exercise, below the toolbar. The Preview button in the toolbar hides and shows it.

### Training
- Machines that show pounds: switch an exercise's details to lb, and its weights show, are typed and step in lb (by 5 lb) in plans, workouts and history, on the iPhone, the Mac and the watch.
- Type a weight instead of stepping to it, with a comma or a point: in a workout on the iPhone and the Mac, and in plans. The watch keeps its stepper.
- On the iPhone and the Mac, an exercise opened during a workout shows how to do it, step by step, under its sets, with a link to a video: your own video link, else a YouTube search for the exercise. Your own description replaces the steps.
- An exercise's details, opened from a plan or the Mac's exercise library, show the catalog's steps for how to do it. On the watch, each exercise's page shows how to do it under its sets.
- Weight steppers only offer weights the gym has, by the exercise's equipment: dumbbells 20 → 22 kg, barbells in 2.5 kg steps, kettlebells in their sizes, machines and cables in 5 kg steps.
- Only one workout runs at a time: finish it before starting another plan.
- Plans take turns: the plan after the one you did last comes first as "Next up", and every plan shows when you last did it.
- Train on the iPhone or the Apple Watch, fully offline. Each set prefills from the set just done, else from last time, else from the plan.
- On the iPhone and the Mac, a workout lists its exercises like GymBook: tick off the next set right in the list, or open an exercise for its details.
- Log a set with one tap, or tick off a whole exercise like "3 × 10 at 20 kg" with one tap; swipe a logged set to delete it.
- Exercise photos show while training, on the watch too, once they're on the device. They download while there is network; in a gym without internet the workout runs as usual and leaves missing photos out.
- Finished workouts go to Health, and the watch records heart rate and calories.
- Train on the Mac too. Its workouts sync like the others but don't go to Health.

### History
- One activity chart shows the last year day by day: gym days in red, and on the iPhone days that reached every daily goal for steps and walking distance in blue, short of them in light blue. Every weekday and month is labeled. Tap a day, or step through the days, to see its workouts, steps and kilometers. Strength workouts from the watch or other apps count as gym days right away. Goals sync through iCloud.
- Statistics per week, month, quarter or year: workouts per period as a chart, and the current period's workouts, sets and volume.
- Move over from GymBook on the Mac: in History, GymBook → Import from GymBook reads GymBook's log export. Match each of its exercises to one from the catalog first, or keep it as your own. Every workout comes in with the sets you did, and each workout name becomes a plan with its last workout's exercises, sets, reps and weight, unless you have a plan by that name. Exercises GymBook last logged in lb show in lb. Importing the same file again adds nothing; cardio and skipped sets stay out. Export for GymBook writes your history in the same format.

### Sync
- Share plans with your training partner with one invite, from Settings → Training partner on the iPhone or the Mac: all plans, now and later, or only the ones you choose from a plan's menu. Shared plans show two people beside their name. Your partner accepts the invite on their iPhone or Mac, and the shared plans join the end of their plan list. Either of you can stop; both keep the plans as they are. Each of you has your own Apple ID, history and weights. Adding, removing, replacing or moving a shared exercise asks whether it's for both of you or only for you, unless Settings says always both. An exercise only one of you does stays in both plans at its place, greyed for the other, and their training skips it.
- Plans, workouts and history sync through iCloud in the background, without opening the app.
- A device without an iCloud account says "Sign in to iCloud to sync" instead of a bare error code.
- The sync status under the plan list, on the iPhone, the watch and the Mac, says "Syncing…" while iCloud is at work and "Waiting to upload" while changes made on that device haven't reached iCloud yet, so "Synced" means nothing is left waiting.
- The sync status shows a sync error until it's resolved, even while the exercise catalog keeps syncing fine. Before, the catalog's next sync could hide a failed sync of plans and workouts behind "Synced".
- Every plan shows whether it's in iCloud, in the plan list on the iPhone and in the Mac's sidebar: a cloud with a checkmark once the changes made to it on that device are uploaded, an orange cloud with an arrow while they still wait. Until then, no other device has them.
- Settings → Training partner → Sync now fetches your partner's plan changes and sends yours right away, on the iPhone and the Mac.
- Training works in every app version. Once a newer version has changed your plans, editing them asks for an update.
- If an update can't read the data on a device, the app says so instead of crashing. It offers to download the data again from iCloud, or to start from scratch on every device.
- On the Mac, back up plans, workouts and your exercises to a folder you pick in Settings → General. The backup is rewritten at launch and whenever something changes or syncs in. Restore replaces everything, on every device, with a backup you choose.
- The watch's info button (i) shows the app version, the commit it was built from and the sync status, and Plan changes lists every plan change that has reached the watch: what changed, in which plan, by whom and when.

### Watch face and Home Screen
- A complication shows today's steps and walking distance. Tapping it opens the watch's Activity: the day's steps and kilometers toward the daily goal, its workouts, and the activity chart of the last year as on the iPhone. Tap a day in the chart to see it. The button with the walking figure opens it too.
- An iPhone widget shows today's walking and running distance and steps, on the Home Screen and the Lock Screen. A ring (round) or a bar shows how far the day got toward the daily goal: kilometers if you set a km goal, else steps. The watch's round complication shows the ring too.

### Look
- An app icon on iPhone, Watch and Mac: a white flexed arm lifting a dumbbell, on navy.

### Languages
- English and German.
- Switch the app between English and German whatever the system language: on the Mac in Settings → General, on the iPhone under the gear on the Train tab. It switches once you reopen the app.
